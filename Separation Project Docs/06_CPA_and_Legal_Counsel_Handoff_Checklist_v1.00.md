# BI-1 / BI-2 Asset Separation
## Accountant and Attorney Handoff Checklist

**Version 1.00 — November 8, 2026**  
**Use:** assemble one secure, read-only transaction package. Give each adviser the materials in the sections marked for that adviser, plus the common package. Do not send full trust instruments, Social Security numbers, bank-account numbers, or unredacted personal records unless the adviser specifically needs them and the delivery method is secure.

## A. Bring this common package to both the accountant and the attorney

- [ ] This checklist and the current **Partnership Asset Separation: Legal and Tax Issue Paper**.
- [ ] One-page executive summary: the objective is a complete separation of the real-estate portfolio with no remaining co-ownership; target economics are 40% for Victor Beraja and his family interests and 60% for Roberto Beraja and his family interests.
- [ ] Current ownership chart for **BI-1** and **BI-2**, including the yellow-column current ownership analysis and the intended 40/60 equalization.
- [ ] “Before and after” entity chart showing the proposed final owners, general partners/managers, and every property-holding LLC or property on each side.
- [ ] A one-page proposed transaction sequence: ownership equalization, asset/LLC allocation, cash settlement, debt/guarantor changes, consents, and post-closing entity changes.
- [ ] The latest Partition Tool property schedule or export, including each selection unit, proposed allocation, fair-market-value support, NPV equity, debt, cash flow, residual tax basis, and planned cash equalization.
- [ ] Current property/LLC inventory cross-referenced to BI-1 or BI-2, address, entity name, and proposed recipient.
- [ ] Valuation support: appraisals, broker opinions, internal valuation workpapers, and the agreed valuation date.
- [ ] List of all planned cash movements: amount, payer, recipient, date, purpose, and whether already paid, funded at closing, deferred, or contingent.
- [ ] Dated transaction timeline for every step already taken or proposed, including prior contributions, distributions, ownership changes, refinancing, and transfers within the prior two years.
- [ ] List of unresolved business decisions and the desired target closing/effective date.

## B. Give the accountant / CPA / tax counsel

### 1. Partnership returns and tax capital

- [ ] Federal Form 1065 returns for BI-1 and BI-2 for the last three tax years, complete with every attachment and every Schedule K-1.
- [ ] Federal and Florida returns for the general-partner LLCs and any property LLC that is not federally disregarded.
- [ ] Tax-basis capital-account schedules by partner for each year provided, including Schedule M-2 and supporting workpapers.
- [ ] Partner-by-partner **outside-basis** schedules, if maintained; otherwise, the records needed to reconstruct them: original contributions, capital contributions, distributions, allocated income/loss, and debt allocations.
- [ ] Current-year trial balance and year-to-date accrual-basis financial statements for BI-1 and BI-2, including bank balances, receivables, payables, reserves, and intercompany balances.
- [ ] Reconciliation of book capital, tax-basis capital, and the partners’ outside basis, if available.

### 2. Property tax basis and depreciation

- [ ] Partition Tool export containing the existing residual tax basis and current-year deduction for each property.
- [ ] Fixed-asset and depreciation ledgers by property/LLC, including placed-in-service dates, original cost, accumulated depreciation, remaining basis, MACRS method/life, bonus depreciation, and current-year deduction.
- [ ] All §704(c) schedules, reverse §704(c) schedules, §743(b) schedules, §734(b) schedules, and any §754 election or revocation documents, if applicable.
- [ ] Prior-year Forms 4562, 4797, 6252, and all other schedules supporting property depreciation, sales, or installment obligations.
- [ ] Workpapers for any cost segregation study, accelerated-depreciation election, bonus depreciation, or special basis adjustment.

### 3. 1031-exchange history

- [ ] Form 8824 for every relevant prior exchange and the corresponding tax-return pages.
- [ ] Exchange agreements, qualified-intermediary agreements, closing statements, identification notices, replacement-property records, and basis roll-forwards.
- [ ] A property-by-property list identifying any 1031 replacement property and the date it was acquired.

### 4. Liabilities, cash, and payment flows

- [ ] Current debt schedule by property/LLC: lender, principal, rate, maturity, collateral, and payoff amount.
- [ ] CPA workpaper calculating each direct and indirect partner’s federal tax share of each **non-recourse** liability immediately before and immediately after closing.
- [ ] Schedule of all partnership debt allocations reported on the most recent K-1s and any supporting liability-allocation workpapers.
- [ ] Proposed settlement-cash calculation, including available partnership cash, reserves that must remain, payment timing, and any deferred amount.
- [ ] Copies of all drafts or side agreements involving money, property, partnership interests, debt assumption, releases, or reimbursement between the owners, trusts, partnerships, or new entities.

### 5. Specific questions for the accountant to answer in writing

- [ ] Is the preferred structure a distribution, a partnership division, a liquidation, an interest transfer, or a combination of steps for federal tax purposes?
- [ ] For each partner, will actual cash plus deemed cash from any non-recourse-liability shift exceed outside basis under the planned steps?
- [ ] Do any prior contributions, §704(c) layers, 1031 histories, depreciation items, or §751 assets cause immediate gain or special allocations?
- [ ] Does any contribution/distribution pairing create a disguised-sale concern, and is disclosure needed?
- [ ] What basis, depreciation, holding-period, and tax-reporting treatment will apply to every post-closing entity and property?
- [ ] Does retaining BI-1 and/or BI-2 provide a material tax or administrative advantage over using new entities, given the final number and identity of tax owners?

## C. Give the attorney, title counsel, and closing counsel

### 1. Entity authority and ownership

- [ ] Executed limited-partnership agreements, all amendments, certificates of limited partnership, and current Florida annual-report/status records for BI-1 and BI-2.
- [ ] Operating agreements, amendments, membership ledgers/certificates, manager records, and current status records for every general-partner and property-holding LLC.
- [ ] Current ownership chart and the proposed 40/60 equalization chart, with each trust and individual owner clearly identified.
- [ ] Written evidence of signing authority: certificates of trust, current trustee incumbency, powers of attorney if any, and proposed consent/signatory list. Provide full trust instruments only if counsel requests them.
- [ ] Prior consents, buy-sell provisions, transfer restrictions, rights of first refusal, or other agreements affecting partnership interests or LLC interests.

### 2. Real estate, title, and contracts

- [ ] Current deed for every property, legal description, title policy, survey, recorded easements, and any current title commitment or lien search.
- [ ] Property/LLC inventory with the present title holder and the proposed grantee or LLC-interest recipient.
- [ ] Material leases, property-management agreements, brokerage agreements, major vendor contracts, permits, litigation/claim notices, and any contracts requiring assignment consent.
- [ ] Insurance policies and any required carrier approvals or named-insured changes.

### 3. Lender and guarantor package

- [ ] Promissory notes, mortgages, loan agreements, guaranties, bad-boy carveout guaranties, amendments, estoppels, UCC filings, and current payoff statements.
- [ ] Lender communications indicating agreement to change the bad-boy guarantor as part of succession planning.
- [ ] Written lender-consent requirements, draft assumption/release documents, and a closing checklist for every loan.
- [ ] List of all guarantors before closing and proposed guarantors after closing.

### 4. Closing and state/local tax review

- [ ] Proposed allocation matrix and cash-settlement calculation.
- [ ] Draft transaction sequence and target effective/recording dates.
- [ ] The CPA’s preliminary tax memorandum or issue list once available.
- [ ] Request for Florida documentary-stamp-tax analysis for each planned deed, assignment, entity-interest transfer, debt modification, and release. Florida considers mortgages and other encumbrances when determining consideration for a real-property transfer. [Florida Department of Revenue](https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/doc_stamp.aspx)
- [ ] Request for draft documents: distribution/division/liquidation agreement; partner, shareholder/member, manager, and trustee consents; assignments of LLC interests and/or deeds; lender-consent and guaranty-change documents; releases; amendments; and post-closing governance documents.

### 5. Specific questions for the attorney to answer in writing

- [ ] What approvals are required under each limited-partnership agreement, LLC operating agreement, and trust authority document?
- [ ] May BI-1 and BI-2 validly make the intended distributions or division while remaining solvent and complying with Florida law and the governing agreements?
- [ ] What is the lowest-risk legal sequence for achieving the 40/60 equalization and complete separation?
- [ ] Which transfer method minimizes title, lender, contract, and Florida documentary-stamp-tax risk?
- [ ] Should BI-1 and BI-2 continue, be divided, be liquidated, or be replaced by new entities after closing, considering both legal continuity and the CPA’s tax analysis?
- [ ] Which documents are needed to release departing parties from management, guaranty, indemnity, and future obligations?

## E. Assembly instructions

1. Create a secure shared folder with three folders: **01 Common**, **02 CPA/Tax**, and **03 Attorney/Closing**.
2. Name files consistently: `BI-1` or `BI-2` + property/LLC + document type + date. Example: `BI-1_2550-S-Douglas_Loan-Agreement_2024-06-01.pdf`.
3. Keep an index with document name, entity/property, date, source, and missing status.
4. Do not change, backdate, or recreate historic documents. Label drafts clearly as drafts.
5. Ask the CPA and attorney to identify missing records and to agree on the transaction sequence before any ownership equalization, cash movement, asset transfer, or guarantor change is implemented.

## Reference points

The checklist requests complete tax returns, K-1s, tax-basis capital records, and liability information because Form 1065 reporting includes partner capital and liability information and can require reconciliation of lower-tier liabilities. [IRS Form 1065 Instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1065). It requests 1031 files because Form 8824 reports qualifying like-kind exchanges and related basis information. [IRS Form 8824 Instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8824).
